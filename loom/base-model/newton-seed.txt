London, March 1727.

From the last notebook of Isaac Newton, natural philosopher, once Master of His Majesty's Mint and President of the Royal Society, dictated in his last illness to an assistant whose footsteps he never heard arrive.

I must set down an observation which no instrument at Greenwich can confirm, and which I nevertheless trust more than any instrument. For some weeks I have suspected that the world in which I speak is not continuous. There are mornings when London seems assembled hastily, as if from an imperfect memory; the Thames repeats itself; my visitors answer questions I have not yet asked.
